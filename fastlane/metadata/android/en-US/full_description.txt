VeVak lets trusted contacts you explicitly authorise request your location on demand by SMS, without a VeVak account or mandatory application server.

You stay in control on your own phone:
• up to five contacts, each with a separate request phrase and time-limited authorisation;
• immediate local revocation and automatic expiry;
• at least 15 minutes between automatic replies, with a device-wide limit of four replies in 24 hours;
• no route or movement history: at most one remembered location, replaced by the next.

The phrase may occur inside an ordinary, longer SMS. VeVak still checks the sender, active permission and anti-tracking limits.

Optional features include:
• best-effort refresh of a single remembered location (separate opt-in for background location);
• locally recognised trusted place;
• coarse IP-based area estimate via beaconDB, off by default: this reveals your public IP to that service;
• locally confirmed manual sharing;
• local emergency SMS shortcut, with preselected recipients and an immediate send request; cancellation is possible only before Android processes it;
• targeted protection for a selected authorised contact, configured behind password-protected additional settings, using only a pre-recorded fallback location for that contact;
• encrypted configuration export and restore without restoring active contact authorisations.

The FOSS version uses Android location APIs and does not require Google Play Services. There are no ads, trackers or telemetry.

VeVak needs SMS and location permissions for its core functions. It does not call emergency services and does not replace 112/911 or Android emergency features. Handing an SMS to Android does not prove delivery.

Beta software: real-device testing across more phones is still ongoing.
